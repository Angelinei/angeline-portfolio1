Read that again.

When I started, the idea was crazy and simple : build 12 product in 12 months. Why, well I'm internationalizing and after met bunch of cool digital nomads in real life (dev, client support, consultants) - parenthesis but I'll really get the impression your destination change the digital nomads you meet - something stayed :

"You need to build something, it's a lifestyle worth to be into"

So first, 100daysofwebmarketing, which led to... stay in my drive. For the best.

I wanted something different, something not free, something which will be a cool real continuity but I wanted to enjoy Bali, Asia and continue my current missions too. Also beginning of AI, travels which highlight the mind, 100schools which started to pivot slowly, so it wasn't an idea to launch but to work on it.
And the time gave me right because recently, one of the godfather of indiehacking launched this cool gamified challenge :

![Marc Lou on X announcing SHIPORDIE.COM: a game where you ship a startup every 30 days or walk the plank, with quests to level up from Noob Pirate](/writing/marclou-shipordie.webp)

So yep, sometimes it's good to just don't nurture the "aversion loss" bias. Because this last initiative taught me something I already observed past months : fast learning is growing. 2 years before, 100 days earlier, 30 days now, and despite the "audience effect", data never lie and on a qualitative way heres the trend :

**Learning have to be project oriented, skill oriented, foundations still matter, but no more only theory and blah blah, education is levelling up.**

And guess what, yes, my building follow the creating trend I identify as "fast learning" democratized currently in EN market (data), brought by AI democratization.

Bref, now its said, let see Shipfast. I bought it almost one year ago (I was learning and during the bootcamp of Codecademy, realized all of the thing - backend + front) would be a pain in the ass without help so **[Shipfaaaast](https://shipfa.st/)** (+ Codefast before + Bootcamp).

![The "Welcome to ShipFast!" email from Marc at CodeFast, August 9, 2025, among Marc Lou's newsletters in the inbox](/writing/shipfast-welcome-email.webp)

Then an idea came… Well, not completely yet. Let’s be honest: I’m still a newbie in “hard” development, and there’s a huge amount to learn, apply, and above all, find the path to ship 8 products in 12 months (this is for marketing, in reality it have been 24-28 months including formation parallal).

On paper, the idea might seem crazy. But only if you don’t look at it creatively. Everything actually rests on the architecture. It forced me to think differently.

As I’m writing these lines, I realize that ShipFast may have inspired me more than I thought. Think about it: a boilerplate that allows you, based on the same foundations (even if the components change), to launch an infinite number of websites. That’s pure replication.

Why not apply exactly the same principle to my idea, which already follows the logic of “1 feature = 1 product”?

1 platform = 1 product. The key lies in the correlation. Even the free tools redirect back to the core of the system. The goal isn’t to build everything at once, but to progressively create an ecosystem that feeds itself (we’ll talk about the Openclaw experiment later).

And that, ladies and gentlemen, is apparently (I’m not the one saying it) “thinking like an engineer, in architectures.” Which is pretty funny, given my background. I’m only now discovering that I already think like an engineer. All that’s left is to learn, apply, and try. Even without aiming for money right away. We’re talking a bit about neuroplasticity here.
