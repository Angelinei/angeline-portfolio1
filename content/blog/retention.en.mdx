Yes, I know, we are not on a high quality prompt here, and for a good reason : memory.md.

Retention is one of the hardest parts of any subscription product. For a learning platform, the challenge is even bigger : motivation drops quickly, and users often start strong then disappear.

Here are some worth testing powered by my co-founder (one of) : Grokie. We’ll gonna test each one progressively and fast, so we can know what it’s worth to invest into and what is not.

### 1. Retention through gamification : XP introduction

A simple and effective lever:

- Give extra credits every month to active subscribers
- Example:
  - Month 1–2 → +10 credits
  - Month 3–5 → +20 credits
  - Month 6+ → +30 credits

This quietly rewards users for staying without aggressive discounts.

### 2. Learning Streaks

Streaks work particularly well for learning products.

Examples:

- 3-day streak → small credit bonus
- 7-day streak → free short path or extra credits
- 30-day streak → badge + larger reward

The goal is not gamification for its own sake, but to create continuity.

### 3. Collection Feeling

Humans like collecting.

Possible systems:

- Skills collected as cards
- Completed paths stored in a personal library
- Visual skill tree that fills over time

This makes progress tangible.

### 4. Smart Next-Step Recommendations

One of the biggest drop-off moments happens when a path ends.

A simple recommendation system helps:

- “Based on your last path, here’s what you should learn next”
- Suggest a logical next skill automatically

This reduces the “what now?” friction.

### 5. Useful Retention Emails

Avoid pure promotional emails. Focus on progress and unfinished value:

- “You’re 2 days away from finishing your path”
- “Your 7-day streak is about to break”
- “You still have 80 credits left this month”

These emails work because they are relevant, not pushy.

### 6. Soft Social Proof / Competition

Without becoming toxic :

- “Top learners this week”
- Weekly challenge: finish 1 path
- Optional public profile with avatar + badges

Social visibility increases commitment. Marc Lou have a leaderboard. Great inspiration.

### 7. Lightweight Community (Later Stage)

Not essential at the beginning, but powerful later:

- Private space for active learners
- Monthly challenges (“Finish 2 paths this month”)
- Ability to share completed paths

Community creates accountability.

### 8. Best Early Stack (Practical)

For a first version, the most effective mix is:

1. **Avatar + visible level**
2. **Streaks with small rewards**
3. **Badges for key milestones**
4. **XP / simple progression**
5. **Credit bonuses for consistency**

Retention is not about locking users in. It’s about helping them continue long enough to see real results. Gamification should reinforce the core promise:

> *Help the user keep going long enough to actually improve.*

If a feature does not support continuity or completion, it is probably unnecessary.
