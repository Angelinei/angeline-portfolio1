I’m mid-debug session on a font that won’t load – five browsers, three CSS approaches, one very stubborn Chrome extension, when it hits me.

We just built a whole workflow around expecting SkillxTube to get things wrong sometimes. Not perfectly. On purpose. It’s called hallucinations limitations and I’m gonna dedicate an article to it.

Here’s the feature : when your learning path has an error – wrong video, off-topic concept, whatever — you flag it, and we regenerate it. Free. No extra credits, no guilt trip, no “are you sure.”

I didn’t design that in a strategy doc. It came out of building the actual thing, watching where the real risk was (someone getting a bad first path and just… leaving), and shipping the fix directly into the product instead of writing a FAQ about it later.

That’s the whole philosophy, honestly. AI’s job at SkillxTube isn’t to know the subject better than the humans who’ve been doing it for years — it’s to point you toward the ones who do, out of the noise of YouTube. Real videos, made by real people who’ve actually done the thing. The AI curates. It doesn’t pretend to teach.

And when it gets the curation wrong – because sometimes it will -the product should say so, not hide it. That’s the difference between confidence and unaccountability. A tool that can admit “let’s try that again” is more trustworthy than one that never lets you push back. Also report a problem opened to create a relationship based on trust and taking care of user experience (Adobe lesson).

If you’re building anything with AI right now: the mistakes aren’t the embarrassing part. Building a system that can’t recover from them is.

I’m keeping this one short — mostly because I want to get back to that font bug. But wanted this documented before I lost the thread.
